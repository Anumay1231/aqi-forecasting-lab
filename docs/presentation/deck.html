Choosing the data

From the suggested topics, we picked air pollution

Suggested data ideas

Energy: crude oil, gas, electricity, solarmarket-driven shocks
Bitcoinrandom walk, regime changes
Air pollution, CO₂, temperature, rainfallchosen
Sales forecastingno open long series
Company financials, revenue, profitquarterly, short

Why daily AQI suits classical time series

  • Long and frequent: one value per day for 10+ years, about 2,500 usable days per city.
  • Real structure to find: strong persistence (lag-1 correlation 0.91), a weekly cycle and a large annual monsoon cycle.
  • All five model families make sense: autoregression, shocks that fade, and seasonality.
  • Official and open: published every day by the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB).
  • Useful output: a 1–7 day AQI forecast with health category is something people act on.
Cities. Mumbai is the primary city. Delhi is run through the same pipeline as a contrast: much higher pollution and a stronger seasonal pattern.

Step 05

05Transformations

1 · Log transform (variance)

  • Box-Cox estimated λ = 0.03, effectively 0, so we use the simpler log(AQI).
  • Mean–std correlation: 0.81 → 0.29. Skewness: 0.83 → 0.01.
  • Forecasts come back with exp() (the median AQI); interval ends are transformed the same way and capped at 500.

2 · Differencing (mean)

  • First difference d = 1 and weekly seasonal difference D = 1, s = 7 were tested in step 04.
  • Not required for stationarity, but used inside the ARIMA and SARIMA candidates so the comparison is fair.

06Seasonality and seasonal decomposition

MSTL splits log AQI into trend, a weekly (7-day) and an annual (365-day) seasonal part, and a residual.

ComponentStrength

  • The annual cycle dominates (monsoon washout vs winter inversion).

  • A 365-day SARIMA is impractical; the annual cycle is modelled with Fourier terms instead.

Step 07

07ACF and PACF plots

Reading the plots

  • ACF of log AQI decays slowly: all 40 lags significant, a sign of strong persistence and the annual cycle.
  • PACF of log AQI cuts off after a few lags: significant at 1, 3–6, 8–10 (and 23). This is an AR signature, pointing to p ≈ 10.
  • After differencing, the ACF has a sharp spike at lag 1 and only 5 significant lags, which suggests an MA part with q ≈ 1–3.
Shaded band: 95% limits ±0.046 (n = 1,826).

How the lags were chosen

  • Start from the plots: PACF lags 1–10 significant, so the AR search covers up to 14.
  • Rank by AIC within each family (step 10).
  • Confirm on unseen data: rolling 1, 3 and 7-day forecasts over 2024.
  • Check residuals: Ljung-Box at lags 7, 14 and 28.
Selected lags: AR(10) uses the last 10 days. For Delhi the same search picked SARIMAX with K = 4 Fourier pairs.
Fitted on 2019–2023; nothing from 2024–2025 used in fitting14
Step 10

10AIC and BIC criteria

SARIMAX + Fourier has the lowest AIC (−996.8) and BIC (−947.2): the annual cycle explains real variance.
MA(10) is far behind (AIC −639). AR, ARMA, ARIMA and SARIMA sit within 30 AIC points of each other.
AIC is not comparable across different d, so the final pick uses 2024 forecast RMSE: AR(10) was lowest (18.46 at 1 day).
AIC = 2k − 2 ln L · BIC = k ln n − 2 ln L15

Conclusion

Findings, limitations and next steps

Findings

  • Mumbai AQI is stationary around a strong annual cycle, with heavy day-to-day persistence.
  • AR(10) on log AQI is the selected model: best validation RMSE and white-noise residuals.
  • Adding annual Fourier terms gives the best AIC and the best long-horizon accuracy.
  • For Delhi the gain over naive is large and significant; for Mumbai it is small.

Limitations

  • Only past AQI is used; weather (rain, wind) drives Mumbai's sudden swings.
  • Residuals have heavy tails, so extreme days are under-predicted.
  • One test year limits statistical power.

Next steps

  • Add rainfall and wind as exogenous variables (SARIMAX).
  • Extend to more cities from the same 297-city dataset.
Forecasts are statistical estimates for study, not official CPCB forecasts22
